What it does

The work an analyst would otherwise do by hand — pulling collar and assay tables out of a 400-page PDF, working out the coordinate system, desurveying, compositing, estimating — done automatically, for every deposit in the database.

Full models from technical reports

Collar, survey and assay tables are extracted by word position from the filed PDF — multi-page tables, "including" intervals, feet, ppm, oz/t and all. The published resource statement comes with them, so the model's contained metal can be checked against the real one.

Parameters that come from the report

Block size, bulk density, composite length, search range, capping grade and the reporting cut-off are taken from the project's most recent technical report. Where no report exists, from what is typical for that region and commodity. Every number says where it came from — and you can change any of them.

Your own data, private

Upload collars, assays and surveys as CSV, Excel or an Access database. Headers are matched by meaning, units converted, holes desurveyed. Your files are yours: nothing is shared unless you choose to share a model, and the source files are never shared at all.

How it works

Load the drilling

Pick a public deposit, or drop in your own files and say which coordinate system they use. Your holes can be modelled together with the drilling MineModelingPro already holds.

Set the parameters

Start from the report's own estimation recipe, then adjust block size, search radius, estimator, top-cut, density or cut-off. Saving rebuilds the model in a minute or two.

Read the deposit

Orbit the model, colour the holes by grade, slice to a section, move the cut-off and watch tonnes, grade and contained metal move with it.

Who it's for

  • Analysts and funds sizing a deposit before the market does, and sanity-checking what a company's release implies.
  • Explorers who want their own holes in 3D without a seat of mining software or a consultant's turnaround time.
  • Corporate development comparing targets on the same basis — same estimator, same parameters, same units.

What it is not

Grades here are inverse-distance estimates built for visualisation and comparison. They are not a mineral resource estimate, they are not classified, and they are not signed by a qualified person. Every model page shows the published resource beside the estimate precisely so the difference is visible. Verify against the source filing before relying on any figure.
